# Facilitator script

*Say the quoted lines more or less as written, so every session is comparable. Don't teach the game. If the player is stuck for 2 minutes on a task, note it as a fail and give the hint listed in `tasks.md`.*

## 1. Welcome and consent (5 min)

> "Thanks for coming. We're testing the game, not you. There are no wrong answers, and confusion is useful to us. You can stop at any time."

Go through `consent.md` and get it signed. Write the participant code, build SHA, input type (emulated / Muse / camera) and start time at the top of the observation sheet.

## 2. Setup (5 min)

> "This game is played partly with your mind and body. Today you'll use [the keyboard as a pretend headset / a Muse headset]. I won't explain how to play; I want to see what the game teaches you on its own."

- **Emulated headset:** say only "The game will tell you which keys stand in for the headset." Don't name Space, F, Shift or B.
- **Muse:** fit it and check contact (EEG panel → contact), then let them start.

Start the timer when the title screen is on, and note the **setup time** once they're in the first scene.

## 3. Think-aloud play (20–25 min)

> "Please say what you're thinking as you play: what you're trying to do, what you expect, what surprises you."

**Neutral prompts only**, when they go quiet for ~20 s:

- "What are you trying to do right now?"
- "What just happened?"
- "Why do you think that happened?"
- "What would you expect to happen if…?" (only to repeat their own words back)

**Never** say "try pressing…", "you need to…", "that's the…", or confirm whether something was right.

Give tasks from `tasks.md` in order, reading each one aloud. Log on the sheet:

- **task outcome:** success, partial, fail or hinted, plus the time;
- **every confusion moment:** a pause over 10 s, a wrong assumption, "what?", or randomly trying keys;
- **every issue**, with a severity of 1–4 (see the sheet) and a short code (e.g. `fire-target-unclear`).

## 4. Second run (5 min)

> "That's the end of that run. You can play again or stop: your choice."

Note whether they **choose** to replay (that's the replay-intent measure), and if they do, the time to the first meaningful action compared with run 1.

## 5. Survey and interview (5–10 min)

Hand over `survey.md` (paper or form). Then ask:

1. "In your own words, what is this game about?"
2. "How did you feel the game responded to *you*?" (perceived control)
3. "What was the most confusing moment?"
4. "What would make you want to play it again tomorrow?"
5. "Anything else?"

Thank them, remind them of the 3-day follow-up if they opted in, and **reset the browser's site data** before the next participant.
